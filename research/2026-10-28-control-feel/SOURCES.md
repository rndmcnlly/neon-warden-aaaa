# Sources, scope and limits

Retrieved October 28, 2026. **No title was played or instrumented for this report.** “Immediate precision” is a design recommendation consistent with the cited slow-mode mechanics, not a claim that these sources measured acceleration in every game. No claimed developer-endorsed universal inertia convention. Memory alone is not used as evidence.

## Stronger evidence: primary material or developer statements

1. **CAVE, DoDonPachi Resurrection Xbox 360 manual**, printed p.9, “Controls.” [Archived publisher manual PDF](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Microsoft_Xbox_360/manual/Formated/Dodonpachi_Resurrection_-_2010_-_Cave.pdf). Full PDF fetched with curl; it is image-only, so pdftotext returned no useful text. Visually inspected PDF page 6, saved [that spread](manual-controls-page6.png). Exact relevant text: “Tap this button to fire your ship's Shot attack. Hold it down to fire your laser. *While attacking with laser, your ship will move slower.” The right trigger holds automatic shot. This verifies **weapon-linked speed selection**, not hold-direction acceleration or a measured ramp curve. It does not imply that Neon Warden should tie focus to firing: our Writ/scan system differs.

2. **Original R-Type team, 1987 interview**, translated by shmuplations, original publication described as probably Gamest. [Interview](https://shmuplations.com/rtype1/), “R-Type FAQ” and “Development Challenges.” The team states five speed levels, a center-point ship hitbox with enlarged enemy/background hitboxes, and small direction-responsive Force animations. This is a useful primary account of **speed selection and visual response**, with a translation/provenance caveat. It **does not establish ship inertia**. Do not turn “R-Type-like weight” into a factual historical acceleration claim. The Force's autonomous movement is not the ship's control law.

3. **Firi Games, Phoenix 2 support FAQ.** [Developer support](https://firigames.com/phoenix2/support). Explains one finger continually controlling the ship while a second activates abilities. Explicitly says Haptic Touch's long press cannot replace pressure-sensitive 3D Touch because the player already continually holds a finger down. Strong evidence for **not overloading sustained touch duration** with another action. It does not specify ship gain/lag/coast mathematically.

4. **Touhou 6 HTML manual, English fan translation of ZUN's manual.** [Touhou Patch Center](https://www.thpatch.net/wiki/Th06/HTML_Manual/en). Search index exposes “Shift Button: Slow your movement.” Direct fetch returned 403, so this is **search-snippet-only corroboration**, not a fully inspected primary manual. Touhou 6 is not evidence for a stock focus hitbox display; later games differ.

## Specialist documentation and close analysis (secondary)

5. **Touhou: Mountain of Faith gameplay.** [Touhou Wiki](https://en.touhouwiki.net/wiki/Mountain_of_Faith/Gameplay): direct fetch returned 418; search snippets describe Shift slowing movement and revealing a colored hitbox indicator. Importantly the current indexed text warns the dot does **not exactly match** the real hitbox. [Shmups Wiki entry](https://shmups.wiki/library/Mountain_of_Faith) was fetched: Shift is Focus, arrow/numpad movement, character speed/hitbox differences, and focus-linked option formation changes. Confidence: good for focus/shot-state convention, lower for exact rendered marker geometry. For our prototype make our own hitbox marker accurate, do not copy uncertain geometry.

6. **Crimzon Clover.** [Shmups Wiki, Controls and Lock-on Attack](https://shmups.wiki/library/Crimzon_Clover), retrieved revision 38571. Holding lock reduces speed, changes option formation, grows a visible lock-on field; releasing fires homing shots; full locks have a distinct sound. Supports **state-specific silhouette, speed and auditory confirmation**. Not evidence of movement ramping. Avoid conflating lock-on radius with vulnerable hitbox.

7. **Jamestown.** [Overview controls](https://shmups.wiki/library/Jamestown), [ship analysis](https://shmups.wiki/library/Jamestown/Ships). Mouse points to a destination and the ship moves toward it at regular speed: target following is not teleportation. Ship article lists Beam speed 6 unfocused/3 focused, Treason 9/4, other ship variations. Notably **Lazar B** slows while charging/charged, then “slowly accelerate[s] back to its regular speed” while firing the burst laser. This is a documented **specific weapon-state acceleration exception**, not evidence all shmups accelerate while holding direction. Wiki values are its own frame units, not interchangeable with our pixels or a measured curve; the proposed tuning is not derived from them.

8. **ZeroRanger.** [Shmups Wiki, Gameplay Overview](https://shmups.wiki/library/ZeroRanger), retrieved revision 39366. Optional Slow and Auto-fire 3 bindings can be enabled; three weapon buttons, charge/lock-on differences. Supports **explicit optional precision/effort controls**, not a conventional mandatory Shift mode or an inertia claim.

9. **Gradius.** [Shmups Wiki, Weapons](https://shmups.wiki/library/Gradius). Power-meter Speed Up increases ship movement speed and can be purchased up to five times. This distinguishes **persistent selected speed tiers** from “hold direction longer to accelerate.” No acceleration/deceleration measurements in this source.

10. **Ikaruga.** [Shmups Wiki, Gameplay Overview and Controls](https://shmups.wiki/library/Ikaruga). Three-button fire/polarity/energy interface, small hitbox, environmental navigation and polarity mastery. No dedicated Focus control is listed. Tap versus held fire changes shot form. Its navigation is relevant to turn authority, but the fetched source does **not establish analogue magnitude handling, immediate stop, or acceleration constants**. No recommendation here relies on invented Ikaruga inertia. Some speed discussion on that page concerns **homing lasers**, not the player ship.

11. **Sky Force Reloaded mobile.** Carter Dotson, [TouchArcade review, June 3, 2016](https://toucharcade.com/2016/06/03/sky-force-reloaded-review/). Inspected excerpt: one-handed control, action slowing on finger lift, “offset touch controls” and direct “ship moves where your finger is” mode. Close review, **not a developer specification**. Supports a touch-specific regrip/access design, not a universal recommendation to coast after finger lift. No exact motion filter measured.

12. **Phoenix II relative touch.** [GameFAQs Basics guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/iphone/193681-phoenix-ii/faqs/76704/basics). Search excerpt says “The ship follows your relative touch” and re-touching elsewhere does not reposition it. Direct fetch returned 403. This is **snippet-only secondary evidence**; Firi's own FAQ above verifies continuous finger control but not the relative gain. Do not claim full verification of transfer curves or stopping behavior.

## What the evidence does and does not justify

Repeatedly supported: distinct slow/weapon states, selected ship speeds, visible state indicators, explicit input alternatives, touch-specific motor assumptions. **Not established:** a genre rule that duration should increase speed, a standard coast duration, a standard turning radius, or exact response curves for every title above. Those would require instrumented frame-by-frame experiments or source/ROM analysis.

Mechanistic reasoning, separate from citations: micrododging maps short intent pulses to small predictable displacements. Acceleration adds recent-history dependence; coast adds motion after release; vector inertia adds wrong-way travel after reversal. A scalar speed envelope with immediate direction reassignment avoids only the last problem. It does not make coast free, and numerical results cannot establish enjoyment or fairness.
